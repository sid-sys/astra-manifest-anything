# Play Store listing — Maya

Written against the ASO principles in the playbook: the primary keyword goes in
the title, secondary keywords go in the short description, and the long
description is written for a human but carries the keywords Play indexes.

One rule shapes all of it, and it is not an ASO rule: **no claim we cannot
stand behind.** Maya writes stories. It does not deliver outcomes. Copy that
promises money or a returning ex is both a Play policy risk under Misleading
Claims and a direct contradiction of our own Terms, which say in as many words
that nothing here makes an outcome more likely. Every line below is written so
that a reviewer reading the Terms and the listing together finds them saying
the same thing.

---

## Title — 30 characters max

**`Personal Manifestation: Maya`** — 28 characters

The brand is Maya now, and the title puts the keyword first because nobody
searches for a name they have never heard. "Personal Manifestation" is what
someone types; "Maya" is what they remember afterwards.

This is the opposite order from the old title, and the reason is that the old
one had the same problem in reverse: `Maya: Manifestation Diary` led with a
word that ranks for nothing. Leading with the keyword ranks better cold, and
the name still sits in the title where Play indexes it.

"Personal" is doing real work rather than padding. The whole argument of the
app is that the manifestation is written about *your* life from *your* answers,
against competitors handing everyone the same audio — so the one adjective
worth 10 characters is that one.

Alternatives, if a different keyword tests better:

| Title | Chars | Primary keyword |
|---|---|---|
| `Personal Manifestation: Maya` | 28 | personal manifestation |
| `Maya: Personal Manifestation` | 28 | brand-first, same keyword |
| `Manifestation Diary: Maya` | 25 | manifestation diary |
| `Maya: Manifest & Affirmations` | 29 | manifest, affirmations |
| `Law of Attraction: Maya` | 23 | law of attraction |

Play indexes the title most heavily of anything. Do not change it casually
once it starts ranking — a title change resets the signal that took months to
build. Changing it now is the right moment precisely because there is no such
signal yet.

## Short description — 80 characters max

**`Personalized AI manifestation audio: affirmations, visualization & sleep`** — 72 characters

Unchanged by the rename: it carries keywords, not the brand.

Carries the secondary keywords the title does not: *affirmations*,
*visualization*, *sleep*, *audio*, *AI*. No word is repeated from the title,
which would waste the space.

Alternatives:

- `AI manifestation stories & guided visualization for sleep and confidence` (72)
- `Manifest your dream life with personalized audio affirmations & stories` (70)

## Keywords being targeted

description — but worth writing down so the copy above can be checked against

**Secondary:** affirmations, law of attraction, visualization, guided

## Screenshots — the highest-impact thing after the title

Order matters more than polish, and the playbook's A/B result is worth
remembering: professionally redesigned screenshots **lost** to the original
self-made ones. Test before assuming.

Six frames, in this order. Each one has a headline in the app's own voice and a
line under it. The headline is the promise; the subline is the proof. Keep the
phone frame and the real interface — a mockup of a screen nobody can reach is
the fastest way to a one-star review that says "not as shown".

**Two to four words, and a full stop.** These are read at thumbnail size while
somebody scrolls, so a headline that needs a second line has already lost. The
longer versions these replaced — "Hear the life where it already happened" —
read fine on a page and vanish on a phone.

**Nothing here counts users or quotes anybody.** The app this listing is
modelled against leads with "Join 300,000+ others" over three five-star
reviews. Both are off the table until they are true: an invented user count and
invented testimonials are the Misleading Claims section of Play policy, and
they are the exact thing that gets a manifestation app reported.

The images themselves are generated — see `store/screenshots/` and
`tool/shoot_screenshots.mjs`. The headings below are laid over them.

**1 — The player, mid-manifestation, words on screen**

> **Hear it already done.**
> Four minutes, in your name.

The product, and the thing no competitor's screenshot can fake. Use a real one
with a real name in it. This is first because it is the only frame that answers
"what do I actually get".

**2 — Home, with a wish typed into the box**

> **Say it in one line.**
> Maya takes it from there.

Type something specific and human — "I want to wake up in a home that is
actually mine" — not a slogan. The input is what makes the output believable.

**3 — One of the four questions, options on screen**

> **Questions about your wish.**
> Not the same four for everyone.

The differentiator, and the frame to spend the most time getting right. Choose
a question that could only have been asked about that wish — the one about the
street, or the light, or who is there in the morning.

**4 — The Affirm tab, a set of five on screen**

> **Daily affirmations.**
> Free, unlimited, and yours.

Show a set with a real detail in it. "I love coming home to a beautiful space
that reflects my taste, success, and personality" beats any generic line.

**5 — The Library**

> **Replay forever.**
> No listening limit, ever.

The answer to the competitor who caps plays at three a day. A full library
photographs better than an empty one, so generate a few first.

**6 — Profile, showing the streak and the two reminder times**

> **Morning and night.**
> A practice you can keep.

Optional if only five slots are used, but it is the retention story and it
costs nothing to shoot.

Two frames to **not** use: a welcome screen, and the paywall. Neither shows the
product, and both are what a person scrolls past.
